# 2026-08-13 — Making it a game

**Asked for:** "More features. More game. Make it a game worth playing! Make it fun!"

Fourth entry today. Previous: setup, build pass, tooling pass.

## What was actually wrong with it

Millhaven had a village, four NPCs, branching dialogue and seven-odd quests, and it
still wasn't a game. You walked up to someone, clicked through their tree, and a line
of text in the corner changed. Nothing you *did* mattered — the world was scenery and
the quests were a menu.

The missing thing was a loop. So this pass built one: **explore → gather → deliver →
unlock**, plus the two bits of texture that make a small world feel like a place —
a day that passes, and somewhere to go into.

## The loop

**Gatherables.** `AMillhavenPickup`: a little procedural item that spins and bobs, swept
up by walking within 1.7m. Deliberately no collect key — pressing E on each stalk of
wheat is busywork, not interaction. Eight wheat in Aldric's north field, five silverleaf
in the cave, nine pearls in the bay shallows.

Their positions are an **explicit table** rather than a PRNG scatter, and that was a
design choice made for the tooling: explicit positions can be checked. `verify_worldgen`
now asserts wheat is on dry ground, pearls sit in water shallow enough to wade (there is
no swimming, so a pearl in 3m of water is a soft-lock), and silverleaf is genuinely
inside the chamber. A scatter would have been fewer lines and unverifiable.

**Quests you play.** Options can now require items, consume them, and attach a gathering
goal to the quest they start. So:

- *The Everloaf Harvest* — Maren → Aldric → the field → back to Maren. The hand-in
  option only appears once the fifth bundle is in the satchel, and choosing it takes the
  wheat.
- *Silverleaf for Aldric* — only offered after you have actually been inside the cave.
- *Shellwater Pearls* — only offered after you have actually been out to the boat.

Those last two matter more than they look: they make exploration a **key**, not a
sightseeing detour. You cannot talk your way into either.

**Day/night.** 720 real seconds per day. Sun elevation produces one `Daylight` blend and
sun colour, sun intensity, sky light, fog colour and fog density all hang off it — one
number to tune rather than five. The initial state comes from calling the same function
with `0.f`, so there is no separate "daytime defaults" block to drift.

The payoff is Wren's existing line about the cave mouth glowing at midnight. It is now
literally true: a point light there is lit by darkness and out by mid-morning. That
rumour was already in the script; it just needed to be true.

**The cave.** It was a facade with a black slab behind it. It is now a chamber with rock
walls, a roof, crystal clusters and the silverleaf. No floor slab — the ground drops
4.4m across the footprint, so the terrain *is* the floor and you walk down into it. That
also sidestepped the problem that killed the flat-floor idea: no footprint near the cave
is flatter than 3.5m, so any slab would have had terrain poking through it.

**Sprint.** Shift. Trivial, and the world is 180m across.

## On not adding more risk

There was already a large uncompiled change set here. Adding a big feature pass to it is
exactly the thing I said last entry was a bad idea, so the mitigation was to make the
new work *checkable*:

- `check_dialogue.py` now models inventory. It searched **3,080 reachable states** and
  confirms all seven quests are startable and completable, no node strands the player,
  and — new — no quest asks for more of an item than the world contains. That last check
  was proven by deleting five wheat from the table and watching it report the soft-lock.
- `verify_worldgen.py` gained gatherable placement rules and a cave headroom check
  (231cm at the tightest point, against a 220cm floor).
- `render_map.py` draws the pickups and the chamber, so the layout can be eyeballed.

Every new tool rule was tested against deliberately broken input before being trusted,
same as before. The dialogue parser's own guard fired first time — I added
`RequiresItem` to the C++ and it refused to run rather than silently ignoring a field it
didn't know. That guard was worth more than the hour it took to write.

## Still true, still not compiled

No engine here. `check_sources.py` (16 files), `verify_worldgen.py` (9 groups) and
`check_dialogue.py` (7 quests, 3,080 states) all pass. `MillhavenTests.cpp` is still the
first thing to delete if the build fails.

Two new files this pass: `MillhavenPickup.h/.cpp`. They follow the NPC pattern closely —
weak-pointer registry, `UPROPERTY()` on everything, world-filtered reads — so they are
about as low-risk as new actors get here, but they are new actors.

## What I would do next, in order

1. **Build it.** Four passes of unverified work is a lot of inference.
2. **Playtest the loop.** The numbers (5 wheat of 8, 3 leaves of 5, 6 pearls of 9) are
   guesses about pacing. The wheat field is ~20m across; that might be a pleasant
   two-minute errand or a tedious one, and only playing tells you.
3. **B4, save format** — now genuinely urgent. There is real progress to lose: quest
   state *and* an inventory.
4. A reason to be out at night beyond the cave glow. Right now the day/night cycle is
   atmosphere; it could be a mechanic (silverleaf only visible in the dark?).
